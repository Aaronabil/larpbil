# Showing a Phone Map on an ESP32 + TFT Display

My IoT project is a companion display for ojol drivers: an ESP32 with a 2.4-inch TFT touchscreen on the handlebar, showing the map and trip info from the driver's phone — so the driver never has to hold the phone while riding.

## How the Link Works

The link is split by size, and that split matters more than anything else in the design:

- **BLE carries small data and control**: connection status, phone name and battery, notification text, commands, and the hotspot SSID and password.
- **Hotspot Wi-Fi carries the large data**: map images as JPEG frames over TCP, plus internet time.

The phone app captures the map and sends JPEG frames to the ESP32; trip info (remaining distance, speed, ETA) updates every 2 seconds over BLE. The phone's name and battery appear on the ESP32 home screen the moment the phone connects — no typing, just the link doing its job.

## The Bug That Wasn't a Timeout

The map stream was unstable: frames arrived incomplete and the client was disconnected. The obvious fix was to raise the timeout — the firmware timeout had already gone from 200 ms to 1000 ms, and the problem persisted.

Instrumented logs told a different story: the Wi-Fi link itself was weakening. RSSI faded from -60 to -73 dBm while a 16 KB frame took over 10 seconds to arrive and still came back incomplete. No timeout value fixes a link that cannot carry the frame in time.

The real fix is a better link — the ESP32 closer to the phone, or a hotspot — plus lower JPEG quality (40) and a longer interval (2 seconds). Not a bigger timeout.

## Two Things That Trip People Up

- **"Connected" on the LCD proves only the link.** A Bluetooth connection belongs to one app at a time, so a connection made in a test app is not a connection in the real app. The LCD saying "Connected and synced" does not mean any data arrived.
- **Measure before fixing.** RSSI and frame timings beat guessing. The moment I logged the numbers, the timeout theory died and the Wi-Fi theory took its place.
