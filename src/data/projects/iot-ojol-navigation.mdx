# IoT Ojol Navigation: A Companion Display for Ojol Drivers

## Overview

An ESP32 with a 2.4-inch TFT touchscreen that sits on a motorbike handlebar and shows the map and trip info from the driver's phone — so the driver does not have to hold the phone while riding. The phone app (Flutter) sends the map and trip data; the ESP32 displays it.

## What It Does

- **Shows the map**: The phone captures the map and sends it as JPEG frames over Wi-Fi to the ESP32.
- **Shows trip info**: Remaining distance, speed, and ETA update every 2 seconds over BLE.
- **Shows the phone**: The phone's name and battery percentage appear on the ESP32 home screen the moment the phone connects.
- **Shows orders**: Ojol and food-delivery orders can pop up and navigate automatically via the phone's notification listener.

## Why I Built This

Holding a phone while riding is dangerous and impractical. A small dedicated display with the map, ETA, and orders — fed by the phone already in the driver's pocket — solves it with cheap, practical hardware: an ESP32 and a 2.4-inch TFT.

## Tech Stack

### Hardware

- **ESP32 DevKit**: The main board, wired to the TFT with 14 jumper cables
- **2.4-inch TFT (ILI9341, 240x320)**: Display with XPT2046 resistive touch
- **Flutter app**: The companion phone app (map, BLE, notifications)

### Protocols

- **BLE GATT**: Small data and control — phone name, battery, notifications, commands, status
- **Wi-Fi hotspot**: Large data — map JPEG frames and internet time

## Building Experience

### The Map Bug

- The map stream was unstable: frames arrived incomplete and the client was disconnected. The instinct was to raise the timeout — but instrumented logs showed the real cause: the Wi-Fi link was weakening (RSSI fading from -60 to -73 dBm) while a 16 KB frame took over 10 seconds to arrive.
- The fix is a better link (ESP32 closer to the phone, or a hotspot) plus lower JPEG quality and a longer interval — not a bigger timeout.

### The Link Split

- BLE carries small data and control; Wi-Fi carries the large map images. A Bluetooth connection belongs to one app at a time, so a connection made in a test app is not a connection in the real app — the LCD saying "Connected" proves only the link, not that any data arrived.

## Impact & Results

- **Working prototype**: Phone name and battery appear on connect, navigation data streams over BLE, and map frames render — with the Wi-Fi stability fix in progress.
- **Casing planned**: A blackbox enclosure sized from the real TFT PCB measurements (77.18 x 42.72 mm), PETG recommended for motorcycle sun use.

## Future Enhancements

- **Stable map streaming**: Hotspot mode + JPEG quality 40 with a 2-second interval.
- **Compact v2**: ESP32-C2 mini with a 3D-printed case.
- **Auto-accept**: Not possible without an official API — orders will stay display-and-navigate only.

## Key Learnings

- **Measure before fixing**: RSSI and frame timings beat guessing at timeouts.
- **Split the link by size**: Small data over BLE, big data over Wi-Fi.
- **Real boards, real numbers**: The casing is designed from the actual PCB measurements, not assumptions.
